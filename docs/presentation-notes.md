# Speaker Notes — Quarterly Review Deck

*Note: fictional course scenario. Full sentences, written to be said aloud — pairs with [docs/presentation.md](presentation.md). Calibrated to [stakeholders/marcus.md](../stakeholders/marcus.md): lead with the number, don't build up to it, and keep every slide tied to a business outcome.*

*Format: each sentence is on its own line so you can find your place at a glance while presenting, without losing any of the detail.*

## Slide 1 — The Problem

Our 30-day retention has dropped from 44% to 37% over the last two quarters — that's the number I want you to leave this slide with.

This isn't a small fluctuation; it's a steady, seven-point slide, and it's not self-correcting on its own.

The important nuance is where the drop happens: it's not at onboarding, it's after week one, once users have had their first good experience and then get nothing that pulls them back.

Before we talk about what to do about it, I want you to walk away from this slide agreeing on one thing: the problem isn't acquisition or first impressions, it's what happens right after.

## Slide 2 — Why Now

This isn't a new problem, but three independent things came together this cycle that make now the right time to act: user interviews, NPS feedback, and a competitive scan all landed on the same explanation independently, which gives us more confidence than any one of them alone.

On the market side, none of our direct competitors — YNAB, Copilot Money, Rocket Money, Monarch Money — currently own this specific gap, so there's real white space if we move.

The cost of waiting isn't abstract: the retention slide is already seven points deep with no sign of leveling off, so every quarter we don't act, we're compounding both the metric loss and the market opportunity we're leaving open.

I want you to leave this slide believing the timing argument, not just the problem argument.

## Slide 3 — The Proposal

Here's what we're proposing: a personalized weekly summary inside the app, built around one spending insight, one nudge that doubles as the goal-setting action itself, and visible progress toward that goal — deliberately designed to deliver something meaningful in the first one to two weeks rather than waiting on months of accumulated data.

I want to be equally clear about what this isn't, because I don't want to oversell it: it's not a debt-aware financial coach, we've explicitly scoped that out for now; it's not just a nicer version of our existing email, we considered that as a lower-effort option and rejected it as insufficient; and it's not a finished, guaranteed solution — it's a tested experiment, and I'll show you exactly how tested in a minute.

The takeaway I want here is precision: you should walk away knowing exactly what we're building and, just as importantly, what we're deliberately not building yet.

## Slide 4 — Evidence (Part 1: What Users Told Us)

I want to start with what real users actually told us, because the data on the next slide only means something once you've heard where it comes from.

Amara, ten days into using the app, called the spending breakdown "shocking in a useful way" — so this isn't fixing a broken first impression, it's fixing what happens after.

Tom, who churned at week five, told us he switched to YNAB because "at least that feels like it is asking something of me" — that's not a design nitpick, that's a user telling us exactly why passive isn't enough.

And Priya, our closest thing to a power user at eighteen months in, said the thing that actually made it stick for her took about three months to show up, and that most people give up before they get there — which is exactly the timeline problem this feature is built to solve.

Our NPS data independently confirms both of these gaps — one user told us a savings goal they'd set was never mentioned again, and another told us the weekly email is the only thing keeping them engaged because the app itself gives them no reason to open it.

## Slide 5 — Evidence (Part 2: What the Data Shows)

Now here's what happened when we actually built and tested this with real users in a live experiment.

We built and tested an interactive prototype through multiple rounds before this ever touched real users.

The real evidence is the live experiment: users who saw the weekly summary had 76% day-7 retention versus 46% for control, which is statistically significant, and 36% versus 22% at day 30, which is the right size to matter but isn't statistically significant yet at our current sample size — I want to be straight with you about that distinction rather than round it up.

One more data point worth pausing on: engagement with the summary actually grew across the four weeks we measured, from 28% up to 56%, instead of decaying the way a novelty feature usually does.

The takeaway before we move on: the early signal is real and reliable, the later signal is promising but not yet proven, and I'm not going to tell you it's proven when it isn't.

## Slide 6 — The Plan

The honest plan is that we're not ready to commit to a rollout date yet, and I'd rather tell you that directly than make one up — we need more weeks of experiment data to get the day-30 result to statistical significance before we can responsibly commit to scaling it.

Once we have that, the milestone is a full rollout recommendation with a real timeline, not a rough estimate.

I also want to name the biggest risk we found rather than bury it: the data shows this feature moves whether people survive week one, but it does not yet move whether people who already survived week one stick around through day thirty — which means there's a segment of churners this doesn't help yet.

We already have a mitigation plan for that specific gap, a follow-on feature we're calling the week-two continuation loop, but it hasn't been built or tested yet, so I'm flagging it as a real risk, not a solved problem.

What I want you to take away here is that the plan has a clear next milestone and a named risk with an owner, not an open-ended "trust us."

## Slide 7 — The Ask

Here's exactly what I need from you today: approval to continue running the experiment until we reach statistical significance on the day-30 number, and approval to start scoping and validating the week-two retention loop in parallel so we're not sequential about closing that gap.

In exchange, I'll come back to you with a full rollout recommendation and a real timeline as soon as day-30 significance is reached — not before, and not without one.

I'm not asking you to greenlight a full rollout today, because the data doesn't support that ask yet, and I'd rather ask you for the right thing now than ask for too much and walk it back later.

That's the ask — happy to take questions.
