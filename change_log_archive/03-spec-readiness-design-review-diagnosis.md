**2026-08-08** — Analyzed the week-5 weekly-summary experiment data (`data/metric-findings.md`): confirmed goal-setting predicts retention independent of the experiment (34.8% vs 21.6%, cohorts 1-4), found a statistically significant day-7 retention lift (76% vs 46%) and a large but not-yet-significant day-30 lift (36% vs 22%, n=50/arm) for `summary_v1` vs control, and a large open-rate lift that grew over 4 sends (28%→56% vs. flat 4-6% control) — **Rationale:** first real quantitative read on whether the recommended direction is working, not just qualitative/simulated signal; recommendation is to continue and expand the experiment, not yet enough sample on the primary day-30 metric to call it proven — **Owner:** me

**2026-08-08** — Full retention diagnosis (`data/metric-diagnosis.md`): decomposed 30-day retention into a Day-7 gate × Day7→Day30 stickiness tree and found the weekly summary moves only the Day-7 gate (46%→76%), not stickiness (~47-48% unchanged in both arms); traced the weeks 1-4 decline to a 36% drop in avg sessions/user, ruling out channel-mix and goal-rate as explanations; ranked 4 churn hypotheses by evidence already in hand — the intuitive "no goal set" hypothesis is directly contradicted by the data (44%/44% split), platform and acquisition-channel gaps are real and well-evidenced — **Rationale:** first quantitative root-cause pass, not just top-line metrics; recommending platform-friction (Rank 2) as the next sprint's test over the higher-confidence acquisition-channel hypothesis (Rank 1) since it's the one this squad can both test and act on directly — **Owner:** me
