# Interview Synthesis — Nudge User Interviews (3 subjects)

*Source: Priya S. (power user, 18mo), Tom R. (churned, week 5), Amara L. (new, day 10)*
*Note: fictional course scenario.*

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. The initial spending breakdown genuinely lands — for everyone
All three users, regardless of outcome, describe the first-touch experience positively. The problem is not the hook itself.

> "The spending breakdown was shocking in a useful way — I had no idea I was spending that much on subscriptions." — **Amara**

> "I really wanted it to work. I connected three accounts. Set up a budget." — **Tom**

### 2. After onboarding, the experience goes static
Once setup is complete, users describe the app as frozen — no new content, no change over time.

> "After the initial setup it felt like nothing changed. The app just showed me the same dashboard every time." — **Tom**

> "I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day." — **Amara**

### 3. Users want an explicit next step, not just more information
Insight alone doesn't satisfy — users are waiting to be told what to do with what they've learned.

> "I do not really know what to do with that information. I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step." — **Amara**

> "I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on and it never really did." — **Tom**

### 4. Personalized pattern-recognition is the real retention driver — but it's slow to arrive
The thing that actually created stickiness wasn't a feature — it was Nudge surfacing something true and specific about the user's own behavior that they hadn't noticed themselves.

> "The thing that made it stick for me was when I noticed it started surfacing patterns I had not consciously registered — like that I always overspend in the last week of the month." — **Priya**

> "But it took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that." — **Priya**

### 5. Passive insight delivery may not be enough for everyone — some want active accountability
Not all users respond to the same mechanism. Priya's stickiness came from a pull model (she checks it voluntarily); Tom explicitly left for a tool with a push/accountability model.

> "I open Nudge every Sunday morning before I start my week. It has become a ritual." — **Priya**

> "I switched to YNAB because at least that feels like it is asking something of me." — **Tom**

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Pull vs. push engagement:** Priya's loyalty came from passive, self-directed insight-checking that became ritual. Tom explicitly wanted the app to *ask something of him* — an active, accountability-driven model. A single "personalized insight" feature may satisfy one engagement style and not the other.
- **Timeline mismatch:** Priya's real payoff took ~3 months to materialize, and she believes most users churn before reaching it. But the metric under scrutiny is 30-day retention — the exact window she says most people don't survive. What worked for her is, by her own account, too slow to solve the problem as scoped.
- **Same starting point, different outcomes:** Amara and Tom both describe an equally strong initial reaction to the spending breakdown, yet one is on a churn trajectory and the other already stalled by day 10. This suggests the initial "aha" is necessary but not differentiating — the divergence happens entirely in what does or doesn't happen next.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

The gap that kills retention isn't the absence of a "next step" feature in the abstract — it's that the one thing proven to create durable engagement (Priya's personalized pattern-recognition) has, historically, taken ~3 months of accumulated data to surface. Week-1 and week-5 users (Amara, Tom) are being asked to wait for a payoff that arrives, if at all, well outside the 30-day retention window being measured. The actionable implication: the team can't just add *a* next step — it needs to manufacture an early, lower-confidence version of the personalization signal within the first 1-2 weeks (even a provisional, small pattern), rather than waiting for the full 3-month accumulation Priya describes. Without that, even a well-designed nudge feature risks repeating Tom's experience: technically functional, but static and generic in the window that actually determines whether someone stays.
