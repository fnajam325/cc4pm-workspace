# NPS Feedback Findings — Engage v2 Discovery

*Prepared for: Marcus*
*Source: 10 raw NPS comments, Nudge users*
*Note: fictional course scenario.*

## Executive Summary

Complaints cluster tightly around three repeated themes — the app doesn't pull users back on its own, content/nudges don't feel personalized, and the experience goes static after week one. Praise is real but thin: every positive comment appears only once, with no repeated theme. The top 3 actionable issues below are ranked by a combination of frequency and fixability, not frequency alone.

| 1 (tie) | Content/nudges don't feel personalized or relevant | 3 | #4, #8, #9 |

## Praise (Single Mentions — No Repeated Theme)

- Initial spending breakdown/first week has real value: *"The first week was genuinely eye-opening."* (#2)
- Strong affinity for the underlying concept: *"Love the concept."* (#5)
- Weekly email is an effective, working engagement channel: *"The weekly email is the only thing keeping me engaged."* (#10)

Praise is directionally consistent with prior findings (initial breakdown lands well) but doesn't cluster the way complaints do — nothing users love is mentioned more than once.

## Top 3 Actionable Issues

1. **The app itself has no re-engagement mechanism — email is doing that job alone.** Users who still like Nudge (#5, #10) are being pulled back by the weekly email, not the product. Fix: bring the in-app experience up to the standard the email already sets, so the app itself becomes a reason to return.

2. **Nudges must get more precise before they get more frequent.** One irrelevant notification (#4, a $12 coffee alert) was enough for a user to disable notifications entirely. Increasing nudge volume or assertiveness without first solving relevance risks making retention worse, not better.

3. **Stated user intent (goals) isn't being acted on.** A user who set a savings goal got no follow-up on it at all (#6), and users broadly want the app to tell them what to do, not just report what happened (#3). Fix: close the loop on goals and shift from pure reporting toward guidance.
